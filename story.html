Prologue — Why Now

Every enterprise we've talked to has the same quiet fear: the AI vendor they onboard today becomes the AI vendor they can't leave tomorrow — and the customer data, chat logs, and moderation history that made the model useful never really belonged to them in the first place.

That fear is rational. So we built OmniNStack backwards from it: on-premise by default, sovereign where it matters, and owned outright by whoever runs it. The bet only pays off if the underlying stack is good enough to run more than one kind of product. This is the story of the two we built to find out — the first two in what we intend to be a much longer list.

I

Outward — AI Customer Engagement Platform

Reaching a customer is a harder problem than it looks.

Every regional messaging app has its own API, its own failure modes, and its own reasons to silently drop your message. Outward Chat treats that chaos as the actual product problem: campaigns are composed once, then delivered across WhatsApp, KakaoTalk, SMS, Telegram, Viber, Zalo, LINE, WeChat, and RCS, with automatic fallback the moment one channel times out.

Outward Studio handles the content side: an AI copywriting copilot drafts the multi-variant messages, CTAs, and scheduled social posts; a conversational directory layer answers a customer's plain-language question — "any Korean restaurants near me that deliver after 10pm?" — with a multi-turn, clarifying search instead of a keyword match.

The part that's actually hard to copy: Outward closes the loop into the physical world. Coupons issued inside a chat thread redeem in real time at the register — Clover, Toast, OKPOS, Force, and custom kiosks — so a store visit converts a digital send into an attributed offline sale instead of a metric nobody trusts.

II

Contexa — AI Trust & Safety Platform

The false positive nobody's accuracy metric can see.

Moderation has a dirty secret: keyword filters and off-the-shelf classifiers look at tokens, not context. A slur inside a news quote, a reclaimed word, satire, counter-speech — a naive system flags all of it as a violation, and because these cases are statistically rare, aggregate accuracy numbers bury the failure completely.

Rather than build the whole platform first and hope the hard part works, we built KO-CTX — a hand-authored Korean "context-trap" benchmark, written item by item by a single author, with zero crawled or template-expanded data, covering six distinct context-trap categories plus hard negatives.

Five scoring approaches are compared side by side — keyword matching, a KcELECTRA classifier, zero-shot LLM judging, a structured-output ablation, and a full LLM context-reasoner — using paired McNemar tests, paired bootstrap, and Wilson confidence intervals, not a single accuracy headline.

We pre-registered the pass/fail gate before running a single score. At this sample size, the most likely honest outcome is "inconclusive" — and we say so up front, because a trust & safety company that can't be trusted with its own benchmark has no business selling one.

III

The Throughline

Same nervous system. Opposite ends of the pipe.

Outward pushes the right content out; Contexa stops the wrong content from coming in. Different customers, different teams, different regulatory pressure — but underneath both sits the same five-layer sovereign stack, engineered once and reused twice.

01. Presentation

Chat UI, dashboards, and native IDE plugins for human-AI collaboration.

02. Governance

Policy-as-code (OPA) and an immutable, identity-aware audit trail on every request.

03. Orchestration

Agentic reasoning that routes both omnichannel campaigns and moderation decisions.

04. Data

Sovereign RAG with secure vector stores — zero data leakage.

05. Infrastructure

Self-hosted and hardware-agnostic, from edge nodes to sovereign server racks.

The reusable core is intentionally narrow and hard to fake: an agent coordination engine that plans, delegates, and self-corrects across long-running workflows; a sovereign memory & retrieval layer that answers with citations and never lets data leave the building; and edge inference optimization tuned for NVIDIA TensorRT and Apple MLX, so the same reasoning runs on hardware an enterprise already owns.
