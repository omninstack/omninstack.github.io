Technical Whitepaper

Contexa

A Context-Aware Detection Pipeline for Cyberbullying and Policy Violations

JULY 2026 | VERSION 1.0 | OMNINSTACK CORE TEAM

Executive Summary

Hate speech, harassment, and cyberbullying keep mutating faster than keyword filters can keep up, while manual review teams can't keep pace with volume — and every blocking decision needs a defensible paper trail. Off-the-shelf classifiers make it worse: they flag the token, not the context, so quotes, satire, counter-speech, and victims calling out their own harassment get blocked right alongside the abuse.

Contexa is OmniNStack's AI Trust & Safety Platform, purpose-built to catch cyberbullying as it happens — a layered detection pipeline that scores policy-violation risk in real time and routes every decision into a fully logged evidence trail. This document describes that pipeline in technical depth.

The Platform at a Glance

Contexa unifies four capabilities that off-the-shelf moderation tools handle separately, or not at all:

  • Context-Aware Classification: A rule engine, AI classifier, and LLM context analyzer working in sequence, so quotes and counter-speech aren't blocked alongside real violations.
  • Multi-Category Risk Scoring: Every piece of content scored 0–100 across eight policy categories, led by cyberbullying and harassment.
  • Automated Decision Routing: Allow, monitor, review, or block — decided consistently by risk tier, not moderator judgment call.
  • Evidence & Trust Score Ledger: A full, searchable detection history and per-user trust score that stands up to disputes.

I. The Challenge: Why Keyword Filters Miss Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is rarely one flagged word. It's a pattern aimed at one person — repeated targeting, pile-ons, coded slang, and intimidation that mutates faster than a static wordlist can track. Two failure modes compound the problem:

  • Pattern Blindness: A one-shot keyword filter evaluates each message in isolation. Ten individually-mild messages piling onto the same target — the actual bullying pattern — never trips a single-message threshold.
  • Context Blindness: Classifiers that flag the token, not the meaning, block quotes, satire, and counter-speech alongside the violations they were quoting — so a victim reporting their own harassment gets blocked next to their harasser.

Every blocking decision also needs to be defensible after the fact. "The AI flagged it" is not a legal or policy defense when a user disputes a block.

II. Architecture: The Detection & Decision Pipeline

Contexa layers detection in five stages, from raw content to a routed, logged decision.

01. Ingestion Layer

Normalizes posts, comments, chat, nicknames, and profile text from across community surfaces into one scoring pipeline.

02. Rule Engine

Catches known terms and evasion patterns — character-splitting, initialisms, lookalike substitution — with dynamic rulesets moderators update without a model retrain.

03. AI Classifier

A KoBERT-class model scores content across eight policy categories with per-category confidence, not a single pass/fail flag.

04. LLM Context Analyzer

Refines the classifier's output for the cases that need judgment — distinguishing quotes, satire, and counter-speech from genuine violations.

05. Decision Router

Resolves every score to allow / monitor / review / block, and writes the full decision chain to the evidence ledger.

III. Cyberbullying Detection in Depth

Cyberbullying is the category the rest of this pipeline is built around. Four mechanics do the actual work:

  • Pattern, Not Just Profanity: Contexa tracks repeated targeting of the same user across posts, comments, and chat over time — not just the content of a single message in isolation.
  • Pile-On Detection: When multiple accounts target one person in a short window, real-time volume spikes combined with per-user trust scoring flag the coordinated behavior as a distinct signal from any individual message.
  • Coded & Evolving Slang: Bullying language shifts to dodge filters — deliberate misspellings, abbreviations, in-group slang. Dynamic rulesets let moderators register new terms the moment they appear.
  • Protecting Victims, Not Just Flagging Them: When a target quotes the bullying to report it, the LLM context analyzer distinguishes that from the original harassment, so victims speaking up don't get blocked alongside their harassers.

IV. Evidence, Audit & Defensibility

Every detection and every moderator action is written to a searchable evidence log, because a dispute needs more than a flag to stand behind.

V. Operator Dashboard & Governance

Admins, moderators, and read-only stakeholders each get exactly the access they need:

  • Live Detection Feed & Review Queue: Real-time violation stream, risk distribution, and repeat-offender rankings feeding an approve/remove/block/permanently-block queue.
  • Role-Based Access: Admins manage policy and retraining triggers, moderators work the queue, viewers get read-only analytics — every admin action captured in its own audit log.
  • Sub-Second Targets: Built for real-time posting experiences, so moderation scoring doesn't become the bottleneck in a comment or chat flow.

Catch the Pattern, Not Just the Word.

Contexa turns cyberbullying detection from a manual, inconsistent chore into an auditable, real-time pipeline — one that protects targets without silencing the people speaking up.
