Executive Summary

Businesses have moved their marketing into instant messaging — WhatsApp, KakaoTalk, Telegram, SMS — because that's where customers already are. But managing campaigns across those channels is fragmented, and proving that a digital message actually drove a physical, in-store purchase has historically been close to impossible.

Outward is OmniNStack's AI Customer Engagement Platform: an omnichannel messaging system with AI-generated content, resilient cross-channel failover, and real-time offline POS/coupon attribution built in from the ground up. This document describes the architecture that makes that attribution possible, and the AI and reliability engineering underneath it.

The Platform at a Glance

Outward unifies four capabilities that are normally handled by separate, disconnected tools, across two purpose-built modules — Outward Studio for AI-driven content and copy, and Outward Chat for omnichannel messaging, in-chat commerce, and attribution:

  • Omnichannel Delivery & Failover (Outward Chat): One API surface across WhatsApp, KakaoTalk, Telegram, LINE, Viber, Zalo, WeChat, SMS, and RCS, with automated cross-channel fallback when a message doesn't land.
  • AI Copywriting, Social Content & Conversational RAG (Outward Studio): LLM-generated message variants, automated social post generation and scheduling across SNS channels, and a retrieval-augmented search layer that lets customers find stores and products in natural language.
  • Offline POS Attribution (Outward Chat): Coupon codes and QR redemptions matched back to the specific message or campaign that drove the visit, closing the loop from digital send to physical sale.
  • Enterprise-Grade Reliability: An asynchronous, queue-backed ingestion pipeline built for high-volume webhook traffic without dropped messages.

I. The Challenge: The Offline Attribution Gap

Mobile-first customers expect brands to reach them on the messaging apps they already use. But two structural problems get in the way of doing that well:

  • Channel Fragmentation: Running campaigns across WhatsApp, KakaoTalk, Telegram, and SMS independently means separate tools, separate delivery guarantees, and no unified failover when one channel fails to deliver.
  • The Attribution Black Box: Once a coupon or message goes out, most platforms have no way to connect it to what happened at the register. Marketing teams can measure open rates and redemption counts, but not which specific campaign variant, sent through which channel, drove a specific in-store sale.

Off-the-shelf messaging tools solve delivery. They don't solve attribution — and attribution is the difference between a marketing spend you can defend and one you're guessing about.

II. Architecture: The Omnichannel Attribution Pipeline

Outward is built as a five-stage pipeline, from inbound webhook to closed-loop attribution ledger.

01. Channel Gateway

A FastAPI request-routing layer handling inbound webhooks across nine messaging channels — including KakaoTalk callback verification and secret-token validation — normalized into one internal event schema.

02. AI Copy & RAG Layer

Generates multi-variant message copy and answers natural-language product/directory queries via vector search over the catalog, with every candidate re-verified against PostgreSQL before it reaches a customer — see Section III.

03. Failover Routing

Rule-based cross-channel fallback — e.g. WhatsApp first, SMS after 15 minutes without delivery confirmation.

04. POS Attribution Layer

Matches coupon/QR redemptions at Clover, Toast, OKPOS, and Force terminals back to the originating message and campaign variant.

05. Analytics & Alerting

Real-time campaign-to-revenue reporting, plus system health telemetry routed to engineering on provider callback failures.

III. AI-Powered Engagement (Outward Studio)

Three AI capabilities sit directly in the customer-facing path, not bolted on as an afterthought:

  • Copywriting Copilot: Generates multi-variant message copy, headlines, and calls-to-action tuned per channel and brand voice, so campaigns don't rely on a single hand-written template.
  • Social Content Studio: Generates and schedules multi-modal social posts (text, graphics, localized promotions) directly across major SNS channels, giving businesses without a dedicated marketing team a continuous social presence.
  • Conversational Directory Search: Customers ask in natural language — "find a Korean restaurant near me serving Jjajangmyeon" — and get an answer grounded in vector search (pgvector/Qdrant) over the catalog, filtered by geography.
  • Alternative Suggestion Engine: When an exact match isn't available, the same retrieval layer proposes the nearest relevant alternative rather than returning an empty result.

Why generic LLM chatbots fail at this, and what Outward does differently: an LLM answering from its own generation alone is fluent but ungrounded — it can describe a product that's out of stock, quote a price that changed yesterday, or confidently invent a store location that doesn't exist. Outward treats the language model as a recall layer, never the final word.

1. Natural-Language Query

"Is there a café latte at the Hongdae store?" — parsed for intent and any explicit store, product, price, or category conditions.

2. RAG Candidate Recall

Vector search returns semantically relevant candidates (store/product IDs) — a shortlist, not an answer.

3. PostgreSQL Re-Verification

Every candidate is re-checked against the live database for current price, stock, and sale status before it's allowed into a response.

4. Grounded Response

The customer gets an answer that matches what's actually true in the system of record at that moment — not what sounded plausible.

The operating principle: Vector DB is a recall layer; PostgreSQL is the truth layer. Semantic search is good at finding what's probably relevant; it is the wrong tool for confirming what's currently true. Outward never lets the former substitute for the latter.

Context that knows when not to reuse itself: a follow-up like "is that still available?" or "what's the price?" correctly reuses the previously resolved product context. But a query that introduces a new explicit condition — a different store, product, price ceiling, or category — is never answered from stale context; it triggers a fresh lookup instead. Getting this distinction wrong is a common failure mode in conversational systems: either every turn re-searches from scratch (slow, and loses conversational continuity), or context is over-applied and customers get answers about the wrong store or an outdated price.
